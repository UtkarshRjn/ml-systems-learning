LoRA — Low-Rank Adaptation, Explained

An interactive tutorial covering the original 2021 paper, the QLoRA/DoRA/PiSSA/rsLoRA/LoRA+/VeRA/LoftQ/AdaLoRA/LongLoRA/S-LoRA follow-ups, and the empirical consensus every ML engineer should know in 2025–2026.

1. The one-sentence pitch

LoRA lets you fine-tune a giant frozen model by training a tiny additive patch on top of it — the patch is stored as two skinny matrices that multiply to something the size of a real weight matrix, but has orders of magnitude fewer parameters.

Imagine a 500-page technical book you didn't write. You want to adapt it for a new audience, but you're not allowed to reprint it. So you tape a stack of thin errata slips to the cover. Each slip says "on page X, replace paragraph Y with this." The book (frozen base model) is unchanged; your slips (LoRA adapters) are small, cheap to store, easy to swap, and can be "merged" into a reprint when you're ready to ship.

2. Why not just full fine-tuning?

Full fine-tuning updates every parameter of a pretrained model. That's straightforward but comes with three growing pains as models scale up:

Storage per task
Full model
Optimizer state (Adam)
2× params in fp32
Serving many adapters
One model per user

For a 65B model, full FT with Adam in bf16 needs roughly:

  • Weights: 130 GB (bf16)
  • Gradients: 130 GB
  • Adam momentum + variance: 520 GB (fp32, two copies)
  • Activations: tens of GB (checkpointed)

Total: > 800 GB just for training state. That's an 8×A100-80GB node minimum, and you get one fine-tune, one model. LoRA collapses this to a single 48 GB GPU. QLoRA takes it further.

Full FT is fine when compute is unconstrained and you're building one model. LoRA shines when you're (a) memory-constrained, (b) building many task-specific variants of the same base, or (c) want to preserve out-of-domain behavior.

3. The core idea in one equation

Take a pretrained weight matrix $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$. LoRA freezes $W_0$ and adds a low-rank update:

$$ W \;=\; W_0 \;+\; \Delta W \;=\; W_0 \;+\; \frac{\alpha}{r}\, B A $$

where $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$, $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k}$, and the rank $r \ll \min(d, k)$ (typically 8, 16, 32, or 64). Only $A$ and $B$ are trained; $W_0$ never moves.

The forward pass becomes:

$$ h \;=\; W_0\, x \;+\; \tfrac{\alpha}{r}\, B (A x) $$

Two matrix products instead of one. The extra path costs $O(dr + rk)$ instead of $O(dk)$ — trivial when $r \ll d$.

Initialization: the trick that makes it start from the base model

  • $A$: initialized to small random values (Kaiming-uniform in PEFT, Gaussian in the paper).
  • $B$: initialized to zero.

So at step 0, $\Delta W = B A = 0$, meaning the model behaves exactly like the pretrained base. This is critical — it lets you plug LoRA in without breaking anything, and the adapter smoothly ramps up from an identity map.

Common student misconception: "$A$ is the zero one, right?" No — $B$ is zero, $A$ is random. If both were zero, the gradient of $BA$ w.r.t. either would be zero and neither would ever move. If both were random, the model would immediately diverge from the base.

Where the $\alpha/r$ scaling comes from

$\alpha$ is a fixed hyperparameter (not learned) that plays the role of a per-adapter learning rate multiplier. The reason for dividing by $r$: when you increase rank, the raw output of $BA$ tends to grow, so scaling by $1/r$ (roughly) keeps the perturbation magnitude comparable across ranks. Whether $1/r$ is the right normalization is a subtle question — Section 6 shows it isn't, and rsLoRA fixes it.

For Llama-3 70B with r=16 on all-linear layers, LoRA typically trains around 0.1% of the parameters. Together with 4-bit quantization (QLoRA), the whole training run fits on a single 48GB A6000 or even 2× consumer 3090s with FSDP+QLoRA.

5. Rank — the most-discussed hyperparameter

The rank $r$ controls how expressive the adapter can be. In the original LoRA paper, the rank ablation on GPT-3 175B (WikiSQL) is striking:

Rank 1 is competitive with rank 64. This is the empirical basis for the "adaptation lives in a low-dimensional subspace" hypothesis. Note: this is on fine-tuning — Biderman et al. (2024) show that full-FT trajectories have effective rank 10–100× larger than typical LoRA r=16, so the low-rank story is task-relative, not universal.

Practical rank choices (2025–2026 consensus)

6. Alpha, and the rsLoRA fix

$\alpha$ is a scalar that multiplies the adapter output: $h = W_0 x + \tfrac{\alpha}{r} BA x$. Two conventions coexist in the wild:

  • $\alpha = r$ → effective scaling of 1. QLoRA convention.
  • $\alpha = 2r$ → effective scaling of 2. "Raschka rule", Unsloth default.

Why the $1/r$ divisor is wrong at large rank

Kalajdzievski (2023) showed the $\alpha/r$ scaling causes gradients through $A$ and $B$ to shrink as $r$ grows — so LoRA doesn't actually benefit from a larger rank when it should. The fix (rsLoRA, rank-stabilized LoRA) is one character:

$$ \text{LoRA:}\quad \frac{\alpha}{r}\,BA \qquad\qquad \text{rsLoRA:}\quad \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{r}}\,BA $$

Effective scaling multiplier at inference

α / r (LoRA)
—
α / √r (rsLoRA)
—

Slide r up and watch the LoRA scale collapse toward zero while rsLoRA decays gently — that's why high-rank vanilla LoRA underdelivers.

7. Which modules to target

The original paper adapted only W_q and W_v. The modern consensus has moved:

EraRecommendationReason
2021 (Hu et al.)Q, V onlyParameter budget tight; that combo won their ablation.
2023 (QLoRA)All linear layersEnabled by 4-bit quantization; consistently better.
2024 (Biderman)All linear layers ≫ attention-only"Target modules matters more than rank."
2025 (Thinking Machines)MLP > attention if you must chooseAttention-only LoRA underperforms MLP-only.
If you show one plot from the modern LoRA literature, show that MLP-only LoRA beats attention-only LoRA at matched parameter budget. This flips a decade of "attention is where the interesting stuff happens" intuition. The practical takeaway is even simpler: just target all linear layers (in PEFT: target_modules="all-linear").

8. Learning rate, batch size, dropout

Learning rate

LoRA is very LR-sensitive. Biderman et al.: "we cannot find alternative learning rates achieving even 90% of the best LR's performance." Sweep it.

RegimeTypical LR
LoRA / QLoRA SFT2e-4 (range 1e-4 to 3e-4)
LoRA for RL (DPO / GRPO)5e-6
Rule of thumbLoRA LR ≈ 10 × full-FT LR

The 10× rule (Thinking Machines 2025) is roughly rank-independent — the $\alpha/r$ scaling absorbs the rank dependence, so you don't have to re-tune per rank.

LoRA+ — different LRs for A and B

Hayou et al. (2024) argue $A$ and $B$ play asymmetric roles at large embedding width, so they should use different learning rates: $\text{lr}_B = \lambda \cdot \text{lr}_A$, with $\lambda \approx 16$. Reports 1–2% accuracy gains and up to ~2× speedup. In PEFT: LoraPlusConfig(loraplus_lr_ratio=16).

Batch size

LoRA tolerates smaller batches than full FT (a quirk of the product parameterization). Very large batches actually hurt LoRA more than full FT (Thinking Machines).

Dropout

LoRA dropout is an "unreliable regularizer" for short runs (Unsloth). Default dropout=0 is fine — contradicts many older tutorials that mechanically copy dropout=0.1.

9. QLoRA — 4-bit + LoRA = training a 65B model on one GPU

QLoRA (Dettmers et al., 2023) is what most people mean when they say "LoRA fine-tuning" today. The pretrained weights are quantized to 4 bits and never dequantized to storage; only small LoRA adapters are trained in bf16.

Three ingredients

  1. NF4 ("4-bit NormalFloat") — a 4-bit datatype whose 16 quantization levels are the quantiles of a standard Normal, information-theoretically optimal for Gaussian-distributed weights.
  2. Double quantization — quantize the quantization constants too. Saves ~0.37 bits per parameter.
  3. Paged optimizers — use NVIDIA unified memory to swap Adam optimizer state to CPU when a gradient-checkpointing spike would OOM the GPU.
The unsung hero of QLoRA is paged optimizers, not NF4. NF4 gets the headlines, but the reason single-GPU 65B training is reliable (doesn't crash mid-run) is paged Adam.

Choose your init: PiSSA and LoftQ

Two clean drop-in improvements to QLoRA's random adapter init:

  • PiSSA (Meng et al. 2024): initialize $A, B$ from the top-$r$ SVD of $W_0$ so the "important" subspace is trained from the start. On Mistral-7B GSM8K: 72.9% vs LoRA 67.7% (+5.2pp). In PEFT: init_lora_weights="pissa".
  • LoftQ (Li et al. 2023): jointly optimize the 4-bit quantization of $W_0$ and the LoRA init so that $Q(W_0) + BA \approx W_0$, closing QLoRA's quantization gap. Especially useful at 2-bit. In PEFT: init_lora_weights="loftq".

The LoRA family tree

LoRA is a launchpad, not a destination. Here are the follow-ups an ML engineer should recognize, each with the key equation and the headline result.

LoRA — Hu et al. 2021 the original
Microsoft · arXiv:2106.09685 · code

$W = W_0 + \tfrac{\alpha}{r}BA$. Adapts $W_q, W_v$ in the original experiments. GPT-3 175B fine-tuned with 4.7M trainable params (0.01%), matching full FT.

QLoRA — Dettmers, Pagnoni, Holtzman, Zettlemoyer 2023 essential
Univ. Washington · arXiv:2305.14314

NF4 + double-quant + paged optimizers. 65B model on a single 48GB GPU. 99.3% of ChatGPT quality on Vicuna benchmark (Guanaco). Applies LoRA to all linear layers.

DoRA — Liu et al. 2024 high ROI
NVIDIA · arXiv:2402.09353

Decomposes each weight into magnitude (trainable per-column vector) and direction, applies LoRA only to direction:

$W' = \mathbf{m} \odot \dfrac{W_0 + BA}{\|W_0 + BA\|_c}$

Adds ~0.01% params over LoRA. On LLaMA-7B commonsense: +3.7pp vs LoRA. In PEFT: use_dora=True.

LoRA+ — Hayou, Ghosh, Yu 2024
Simons Institute · arXiv:2402.12354

Uses different LRs for $A$ and $B$: $\text{lr}_B = \lambda \cdot \text{lr}_A$ with $\lambda \approx 16$. 1–2% acc gain, up to 2× speedup. In PEFT: LoraPlusConfig.

rsLoRA — Kalajdzievski 2023
Cohere · arXiv:2312.03732

Replaces $\alpha/r$ with $\alpha/\sqrt{r}$. Makes high-rank LoRA actually benefit from more rank. In PEFT: use_rslora=True.

VeRA — Kopiczko, Blankevoort, Asano 2024
Qualcomm AI · arXiv:2310.11454

One frozen random $A, B$ pair shared across all layers; only two per-layer scaling vectors $\mathbf{d}, \mathbf{b}$ are trained:

$h = W_0 x + \Lambda_b B \Lambda_d A x$

~10× fewer params than LoRA at similar quality. Useful when per-user adapter storage dominates.

PiSSA — Meng, Wang, Zhang 2024 drop-in
Peking Univ. · arXiv:2404.02948

Init $A, B$ from the top-r SVD of $W_0$. Trains the principal subspace from the start. On Mistral-7B GSM8K: 72.9% vs LoRA 67.7%. QPiSSA + 4-bit LLaMA-3-70B: 86.05% vs QLoRA 81.73%. In PEFT: init_lora_weights="pissa".

LoftQ — Li et al. 2023 (ICLR 2024)
Microsoft · arXiv:2310.08659

Jointly optimizes 4-bit quantization of $W_0$ and LoRA init so $Q(W_0) + BA \approx W_0$. Especially strong at aggressive 2-bit quantization where QLoRA breaks. In PEFT: init_lora_weights="loftq".

AdaLoRA — Zhang et al. 2023
Georgia Tech · arXiv:2303.10512

Parameterizes $\Delta W = P \Lambda Q$ (SVD form) and adaptively prunes small singular values during training, moving budget to layers that need it. Wins especially at tight parameter budgets.

LongLoRA — Chen et al. 2024 (ICLR Oral)
CUHK / MIT · arXiv:2309.12307

Combines LoRA on attention + trainable norm/embedding + shifted sparse attention (S²-Attn) during training. Extends LLaMA2-7B to 100k context; 70B to 32k on one 8×A100 node. S²-Attn is a two-line trick that reverts to dense attention at inference.

Also worth knowing (shorter mentions)

  • Biderman et al. 2024 — "LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less" (TMLR). The rigorous critical study. arXiv:2405.09673
  • Thinking Machines Lab, "LoRA Without Regret" (Sept 2025) — the definitive modern hyperparameter study, source of the "MLP > attention", "10× LR" and "rank-1 for RL" claims. thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora
  • "An Illusion of Equivalence" (2024) — argues LoRA and full-FT solutions differ structurally even when scores match. arXiv:2410.21228
  • BeamLoRA, NLoRA, QuAILoRA, PLoRA — 2025 initializations and concurrent-training variants worth grep-ing.

Serving LoRA adapters

Two very different deployment scenarios, two different answers.

Case 1: single-tenant (one adapter, one base model)

Just merge and unload. Because $\Delta W = \tfrac{\alpha}{r} BA$ is the same shape as $W_0$, you compute $W_\text{merged} = W_0 + \tfrac{\alpha}{r} BA$ once and store the result as a normal model. Zero inference overhead — this is LoRA's canonical advantage over Houlsby-style adapters which insert extra layers. 

from peft import PeftModel
merged_model = peft_model.merge_and_unload()
merged_model.save_pretrained("./merged")   # ship this like any HF model

Case 2: multi-tenant (thousands of adapters, one base)

Merging destroys multi-tenancy. Once merged, you can't serve a different user's adapter from the same base weights. If you're running an "many custom fine-tunes on one endpoint" product, you need to keep adapters separate.

S-LoRA (MLSys 2024)

Sheng et al. (arXiv:2311.03285) built the reference research system for serving thousands of concurrent LoRA adapters on one GPU. Key ideas:

  • Unified Paging — one memory pool for KV cache and adapter weights, paged like vLLM's PagedAttention. Different adapter ranks coexist without fragmentation.
  • Custom heterogeneous-batch CUDA kernels: MBGMM (multi-batch grouped GEMM for prefill) and MBGMV (for decode).
  • Tensor-parallel strategy tolerant of ranks differing between adapters in the same batch.

Reported up to 4× throughput vs vLLM + HF PEFT, orders of magnitude more concurrent adapters.

Punica

Parallel work (arXiv:2310.18547) with 12× throughput claims via similar fused batched-GEMM ideas.

vLLM multi-LoRA

vLLM has native LoRA-adapter support since 2024 — production-grade path for most teams. Group adapters by rank for kernel efficiency; adapters are hot-swappable per request.

When LoRA falls short

Biderman et al. 2024 ("LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less", arXiv:2405.09673) is required reading:

Continued pretraining (20B tokens code/math)
LoRA lags full FT
Out-of-domain forgetting
LoRA preserves better

Their four practical recommendations, verbatim:

  1. Use LoRA for IFT and not CPT.
  2. Identify the highest LR that enables stable training.
  3. Target all modules.
  4. Choose rank per memory constraints, with 16 being a good choice.
The "learns less AND forgets less" trade-off is a feature. When you want out-of-domain behavior preserved (e.g., a customer-support LoRA that shouldn't destroy the base model's general reasoning), LoRA can be the right answer even when full FT is available.

Quick quiz

Test your intuition. Click your answer to reveal the explanation.

1. In LoRA's $W = W_0 + \tfrac{\alpha}{r}BA$, which matrix is initialized to zero?
$B$ is zero, $A$ is random. This makes $\Delta W = BA = 0$ at step 0 (so the model starts identical to the base) while ensuring gradients still flow — if both were zero, neither would ever update.
2. Your team runs a service with 5,000 LoRA adapters (one per customer) on one Llama-70B base. Should you merge each adapter into the base model?
Merging is single-tenant only. Once you fold an adapter into $W_0$, you can't serve any other adapter from that instance. S-LoRA and vLLM's multi-LoRA path batch requests across many adapters on one shared base.
3. You're doing continued pretraining on 20B tokens of code. Should you use LoRA or full FT?
Biderman et al. 2024 showed LoRA substantially underperforms full FT on continued pretraining for hard skills (code/math). Their explicit recommendation: "Use LoRA for IFT and not CPT."
4. If you have to pick between adapting attention-only or MLP-only at the same parameter budget, which wins?
Thinking Machines' 2025 "LoRA Without Regret" showed MLP-only LoRA beats attention-only at matched budget. The practical takeaway is simpler: just do all-linear, but if forced to choose, MLP.
5. You push rank from 16 to 256 with standard $\alpha/r$ LoRA and quality doesn't improve. Why?
Kalajdzievski (2023) formalized this: vanilla LoRA's $1/r$ divisor causes gradient magnitudes to collapse as $r$ grows, so higher rank contributes nothing. rsLoRA's $1/\sqrt{r}$ scaling fixes it. In PEFT: use_rslora=True.
6. What's the biggest memory savings from QLoRA on a 65B model, roughly?
NF4 quantization compresses weights ~4× vs bf16 (16-bit → 4-bit), double quantization saves another ~0.37 bits/param, and optimizer state becomes tiny because you're training LoRA (millions of params), not the base (billions).
7. What's the "LoRA learns less and forgets less" trade-off in one sentence?
Biderman et al. 2024. LoRA does not fully absorb the task (learns less), but for the same reason it doesn't overwrite unrelated capabilities either (forgets less). Useful when you need to add a skill without regressing others.
8. What's the fastest one-line change that consistently improves QLoRA?
PiSSA initializes $A, B$ from the top-r SVD of $W_0$, so the "important" singular subspace is trained from step 0. Reports several points of GSM8K improvement on Mistral-7B and 4-bit LLaMA-3-70B for a one-line change.

How to run LoRA today (2026)

from peft import LoraConfig, get_peft_model

config = LoraConfig(
    r=16,
    lora_alpha=32,           # α = 2r convention
    target_modules="all-linear",   # not just Q, V
    lora_dropout=0.0,        # keep at 0 unless you have a reason
    bias="none",
    task_type="CAUSAL_LM",
    use_rslora=True,         # α/√r; use if r > 32
    # init_lora_weights="pissa",   # ← the one-line quality upgrade
    # use_dora=True,               # ← another one-line quality upgrade
)
model = get_peft_model(base_model, config)

# ... train ...

# single-tenant: merge for zero inference overhead
merged = model.merge_and_unload()
merged.save_pretrained("./ready-to-serve")
